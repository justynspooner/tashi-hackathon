# Capture the Flag — Park Edition: Complete Ruleset

## Overview
A tag-based, no-shooting Capture the Flag game designed for **8–12 players** in a park setting. Played in timed rounds with a points system to determine a winner across a match.

---

## 1. Equipment Needed

| Item | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| 2 distinct flags (bandanas, bright cloth on a stick) | The objective — must be visible from 3m away |
| Cones or markers × 8+ | Mark: centre line, flag zones, jail zones, field boundaries |
| 1 whistle | Referee signals (start, stop, goals, disputes) |
| Stopwatch / phone timer | Round timing |
| 2 colours of wristband, bandana, or pinnies | Team identification — must be visible at a glance |
| Scorecard + pen | Tracking points |

## 2. Field Setup

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    TEAM A TERRITORY                  │
│                                                      │
│   [JAIL A]                              [FLAG A]     │
│   (3m×3m)                             (in Flag Zone) │
│                                        (2m radius)   │
│                                                      │
├─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─  CENTRE LINE  ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ┤
│                                                      │
│                                                      │
│   [FLAG B]                              [JAIL B]     │
│  (in Flag Zone)                         (3m×3m)      │
│   (2m radius)                                        │
│                                                      │
│                    TEAM B TERRITORY                  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Field Rules
- **Recommended field size:** ~30m × 40m for 8–12 players. A quarter of a soccer pitch or a large section of open park works well. Too big and defenders can never catch anyone; too small and there's no room to manoeuvre.
- **Centre line** must be clearly marked (cones every 5m, or a natural landmark like a path).
- **Flag zone:** A circle of 2m radius around the flag, marked with cones. The flag must be placed upright and visible — not hidden, buried, or obscured.
- **Jail zone:** A clearly marked 3m × 3m area in each team's territory, positioned away from their flag (ideally opposite corner).
- **Boundaries:** The entire field must have agreed outer boundaries. Stepping out of bounds = immediate return to your own side (no penalty, no jail — just reset).

---

## 3. Teams & Roles

- **Split into 2 even teams** of 4–6 players each.
- If odd numbers, the extra player becomes the referee. If even, one person volunteers as referee (or rotate the referee role each round).
- Each team **must wear their team colour** visibly (wristband, bandana, pinnie).
- Each team privately assigns roles (this is strategy, not rules):
  - **Defenders** — guard the flag and tag invaders
  - **Attackers** — cross into enemy territory to capture the flag
  - **Jailbreakers** — specialise in freeing teammates from jail
- With 4–5 per side, most players will need to play multiple roles. That's part of the fun.
- There is **1 neutral referee** (does not play).

---

## 4. Core Rules

### 4.1 Objective
Capture the opposing team's flag and carry it back across the centre line into your own territory.

### 4.2 Tagging
- **You can only tag opponents who are in YOUR territory** (i.e., you are defending).
- A legal tag = **a firm two-handed touch on the torso or shoulders**. No slapping, grabbing clothing, pushing, or tripping.
- **Tag disputes:** If there is any dispute about whether a tag occurred, the referee's call is final. If no referee saw it, the defender's claim stands (advantage to defence to discourage deep solo runs without caution).
- You **cannot** tag someone who is in their own territory.
- You **cannot** tag someone who is in jail or actively being freed (see 4.4).

### 4.3 What Happens When Tagged
- The tagged player must **immediately stop moving** and walk directly to the opposing team's jail zone. No delays, no throwing the flag.
- If the tagged player was **carrying the flag**, the flag is dropped exactly where the tag happened. A defender on that team must return the flag to its flag zone before the next play on that flag. (The flag cannot be moved further from its zone by defenders — only returned to the zone.)

### 4.4 Jail Rules
- Tagged players go to the **opponent's jail zone** and must stay there.
- **Jailbreak:** A free teammate can release **one** jailed player by entering the jail zone and tagging (touching) any one prisoner. Both the rescuer and the freed player then get a **free walk** — they walk back to the centre line without being tagged. They must walk, not run. They raise both hands to signal free walk.
- **Chain rule:** Jailed players may hold hands to form a chain extending from the jail zone. A teammate only needs to tag the end of the chain to free **everyone in the chain**. All freed players + the rescuer get the free walk.
- **No guarding jail from inside the jail zone.** Defenders may guard near the jail but must stay at least **2 metres** from the jail zone boundary. (This prevents making jailbreaks impossible.)
- **Maximum jail time (mercy rule):** If a player has been in jail for **3 consecutive minutes** without a rescue attempt, they are automatically freed and walk back to their side. (Critical for small teams — losing 2 of 5 players to jail for too long makes the game unplayable.)

### 4.5 Flag Rules
- The flag **must** be placed in the flag zone, standing upright, visible from all directions.
- Defenders **cannot stand inside the flag zone**. They must stay at least **1 metre** outside the flag zone boundary. (This prevents camping directly on top of the flag.)
- An attacker who enters the flag zone and **picks up the flag** must attempt to carry it back to their own territory. They may pass the flag to a teammate (see 4.6).
- Once the flag is picked up, the attacker carrying it **cannot throw it**. It must be handed off or carried.
- If the flag carrier is tagged, the flag drops where they were tagged (see 4.3).
- A dropped flag must be **returned to the flag zone by a defender** before it can be "live" again. While a defender is returning it, opponents cannot intercept the flag (it's dead during the return).

### 4.6 Flag Passing
- The flag carrier **may hand the flag** to a teammate within arm's reach. No throwing or tossing.
- A handoff counts as continuous possession — if the new carrier is tagged, normal rules apply.

### 4.7 Safe Zone / Home Territory
- You are **safe in your own territory**. You cannot be tagged there.
- Crossing the centre line into enemy territory makes you vulnerable to being tagged.
- You may retreat back to your own side at any time (you don't have to commit).

## 5. Scoring System

### Per Round
| Event | Points |
|-------|--------|
| **Flag capture** (flag carried across centre line into your territory) | **3 points** |
| **Jailbreak** (freeing 1+ prisoners) | **1 point per jailbreak action** (not per player freed) |

### Match Structure
- A match consists of **3 rounds** (or 5 for a longer session).
- Each round lasts **7 minutes** (sweet spot for 8–12 players — long enough for strategy, short enough to stay intense).
- **Teams switch sides** between rounds (to account for terrain/sun advantage).
- At the end of a round, if no flag has been captured, the round is a **0–0 draw** for flag points (jailbreak points still count).
- If a flag is captured, the round ends **immediately** — the capturing team scores 3 points, play resets.
- If a flag is captured with time remaining, **the clock resets** and a new mini-round begins with the remaining time. Both teams reset to their sides.

### Winning
- Team with the most points at the end of all rounds wins.
- **Tiebreaker:** If tied on points, the team with the most flag captures wins. If still tied, one sudden-death round of 5 minutes is played (first capture wins, no jailbreak points).

---

## 6. Referee Responsibilities

The referee is **neutral and final**:
1. Blows whistle to start and end each round.
2. Resolves all tag disputes.
3. Monitors jail zones and flag zones for illegal guarding (too close).
4. Tracks time and score.
5. Can issue **warnings** for:
   - Rough play (aggressive tags, shoving, tripping)
   - Flag zone camping by defenders
   - Jail zone camping by defenders
   - Delay of game (not going to jail promptly, not returning a dropped flag promptly)
6. **Two warnings = 1-minute penalty sit-out** for the offending player (they sit at their own jail for 1 minute, cannot play).
7. Can pause the game (whistle blast × 3) for injury, dispute, or weather.

---

## 7. Game Flow — How a Typical Round Works

1. **Setup (2 min):** Teams go to their sides, place their flag, discuss strategy.
2. **Whistle — Round starts:** Players may cross the centre line at will.
3. **Mid-game:** Attackers try to reach the enemy flag zone. Defenders tag invaders, sending them to jail. Jailbreakers try to free teammates.
4. **Flag grabbed:** An attacker reaches the flag zone, picks up the flag, and attempts to run it home. Defenders chase. Allies try to screen or distract.
5. **Flag captured OR time expires:** Whistle. Points awarded. Brief reset.
6. **Repeat** for the next round.

---

## 8. Edge Cases & Dispute Resolution

| Situation | Ruling |
|-----------|--------|
| Two defenders tag the same attacker simultaneously | One tag is sufficient — player goes to jail |
| Tag happens right on the centre line | Defender's call stands — if in doubt, the player is considered in enemy territory |
| Flag carrier steps out of bounds | Flag is returned to its flag zone. Carrier goes back to their own side (no jail) |
| A defender accidentally enters the flag zone | Referee warns them. If an attacker grabs the flag during this, the grab stands |
| Player argues with referee | One warning, then 1-minute sit-out. Game continues |
| A freed player runs instead of walking during free walk | They lose free walk protection — can be tagged immediately |
| Attacker grabs flag at exact moment they're tagged | Tag takes priority — flag is dropped, attacker goes to jail |
| Flag falls off its stand due to wind | Defenders replace it. Play continues. No point awarded |
| Both flags captured simultaneously | Both teams score 3 points. Reset |

---

## 9. Pre-Game Briefing Checklist

Before playing, the referee reads aloud and confirms with all players:
- [ ] Boundaries are clear to everyone
- [ ] Tag method demonstrated (two-hand torso touch)
- [ ] Jail location and free-walk rule understood
- [ ] Flag zone no-camping rule understood (1m for defenders)
- [ ] Jail zone no-camping rule understood (2m for defenders)
- [ ] Scoring confirmed (3 pts capture, 1 pt jailbreak)
- [ ] Round time confirmed
- [ ] Number of rounds confirmed
- [ ] Referee is final on all disputes
- [ ] Any optional add-ons agreed upon

## Summary: Why This Works in Practice

1. **No shooting** — purely tag-based, accessible to all fitness levels.
2. **No permanent elimination** — jail + jailbreak keeps everyone playing. Mercy rule prevents anyone sitting out too long.
3. **Anti-camping rules** — buffer zones around flag and jail prevent stalemates.
4. **Points for jailbreaks** — encourages active play even when flag captures are hard. Prevents turtling.
5. **Side-switching** — eliminates terrain advantage complaints.
6. **Neutral referee** — single authority for disputes eliminates arguments.
7. **Clear edge-case table** — pre-answers the most common "but what about..." arguments.
8. **Timed rounds** — prevents games from dragging. Creates urgency.
